# Render Cleaning Idea Assessment

Date: 8 March 2026

## Executive View

The idea is directionally good, but the current version is weaker than the SWC route you already have.

My recommendation is:

1. Do not launch `Somerset Render Cleaning` as a separate standalone brand yet.
2. Strengthen render cleaning inside the existing SWC site first.
3. Only spin out a separate brand later if render cleaning proves it can support its own lead flow, proof base, and operating model.

The main reason is simple: the separate idea throws away the strongest assets you already own, namely trust, reviews, age, insurance signals, existing service infrastructure, and cross-sell potential.

## What Exists Today

### 1. The separate render-cleaning project is only a design note

The `somerset-render-cleaning` repo currently has one commit and one planning document. It is not a built site. The brief is a one-page landing page with placeholder images, placeholder testimonials, no real proof assets, and no email/form wiring beyond a future note.

That is fine as an idea capture, but it means the concept is still very early and not yet strong enough to justify a new brand on its own.

### 2. SWC already has a much stronger base

The existing SWC site already gives render cleaning:

- a dedicated service page
- strong metadata and FAQ coverage
- existing booking and quote plumbing
- 220+ Google reviews
- 5.0 rating
- `£5m` insurance signal
- established year `2019`
- a broader service ecosystem for bundling and repeat work

That is a serious advantage. A new brand would start at zero on all of it.

## Current SWC Gaps To Fix Immediately

SWC is the stronger platform, but it is not fully tightened yet.

I found three obvious gaps:

1. The render page presents `From £200`, but the pricing config marks render cleaning as `quote-required`.
2. The shared service-page layout says "get an instant price", which does not fit a site-visit-led render quote.
3. The main render photo asset is weak at only `48K`, which reduces perceived quality on a highly visual service.

That does not weaken the case for SWC. It strengthens it, because these are fixable page-level issues, not reasons to create a second brand.

## My Main Judgment

The current standalone idea is not wrong. It is just prematurely separate.

If you launch it now as a fresh business identity, you are likely to create four avoidable problems:

1. Lower trust at the point of conversion.
2. More SEO effort for less return.
3. More admin and brand overhead.
4. More difficulty proving specialist credibility without real photos and case studies.

In plain terms: you would be rebuilding from scratch something SWC already largely gives you.

## Where The Current Idea Is Weak

### 1. The strategic position is too isolated

The brief says this should be "a separate business from SWC". That is the biggest weakness in the whole concept.

Right now, SWC gives you:

- review volume
- established operating history
- brand familiarity
- insurance reassurance
- an existing phone number and conversion path
- natural cross-sell into gutters, fascias, conservatory roofs, windows, and pressure washing

Removing render cleaning from that ecosystem makes the offer look more niche, but it also makes it look less proven.

### 2. The site structure is too thin

The proposed site is a single page only. That is acceptable for a paid-traffic landing page, but weak as the full web presence for a specialist local service.

It lacks:

- area-specific pages
- real job case studies
- dedicated process/safety detail
- an evidence-led gallery
- pages for related high-intent questions
- a stronger qualification flow

For a niche service, thinness is a real problem. You need proof, not just polish.

### 3. The proof layer is not there yet

The design relies on placeholder images and placeholder testimonials. That is a conversion problem.

Render cleaning is highly visual. People want to see:

- green and black staining before
- the cleaned result after
- the exact type of property
- the type of render
- reassurance that the surface was treated safely

Without real before/after proof, the site will feel generic no matter how clean the design is.

### 4. The quote form is too shallow

The proposed form only asks for:

- name
- email
- phone
- postcode
- message

That is enough for a general contact form, but not enough for efficient quoting.

For render cleaning, you want qualification data up front:

- property type
- number of storeys
- estimated wall area
- render type if known
- severity of staining
- access constraints
- whether scaffolding/cherry picker may be needed
- whether the customer wants biocide protection
- photo upload

The better the intake, the faster and more confidently you can quote.

### 5. The visual direction is slightly off for the audience

The dark charcoal plus electric accent direction could look sharp, but it is not obviously the best match for a homeowner-led exterior cleaning service.

Render cleaning is a reassurance sale, not a hype sale.

The visuals should say:

- safe
- specialist
- premium
- local
- proven

That usually means letting the before/after photography do the heavy lifting rather than relying on a stylised dark theme.

### 6. The current promise is still too generic

The brief says the service is for "transforming dirty, algae-stained renders". That is broadly true, but the stronger angle is:

- safe specialist cleaning for modern render systems
- no pressure-washing damage
- results that last
- better kerb appeal without repainting
- expert assessment before treatment

That is more concrete and more commercial.

## What Is Already Good

There are solid instincts in the idea:

- render cleaning is treated as a specialist service, not just another add-on
- the brief understands the importance of soft wash vs pressure wash
- it identifies relevant render types like K-rend, monocouche, and silicone render
- it puts before/after proof high on the page
- it keeps secondary services in the background rather than muddying the message

So the problem is not the service positioning itself. The problem is the decision to separate too early and the lack of proof-led execution.

## Best Improvement: Keep It Under SWC, But Reposition It Harder

The best version of this idea is not "generic SWC page with render added".

It is:

`Render Cleaning by Somerset Window Cleaning`

That gives you both:

- the specialist angle
- the parent-brand credibility

This is the route I would take.

## What I Would Change

### 1. Upgrade the positioning

Move from:

"We clean render"

To:

"We specialise in safe soft-wash cleaning for modern rendered homes across Somerset."

The page should lead with:

- safe for K-rend, monocouche, silicone, acrylic, cement and lime render
- no high-pressure damage
- expert assessment first
- optional biocide aftercare
- dramatic kerb-appeal improvement

### 2. Build a proof-first page, not a design-first page

The single biggest improvement would be stronger proof content:

- 6 to 12 real before/after sets
- captions naming area and render type
- at least 2 short case studies
- one close-up process image
- one team-on-site image
- one result image from street view distance

At the moment the actual SWC render image is only `48K`, which supports the internal note that the photo asset is weak. This is a real bottleneck.

I would also align the pricing/message stack so the page does not imply an instant-price experience if the real process is assessment first and quote second.

### 3. Add an expert assessment section

A strong render page should explain that you assess:

- render system
- biological growth type
- staining depth
- access risk
- whether a full clean or treatment-only approach is best

This makes the service feel specialist and helps justify premium pricing.

### 4. Make the offer more operationally specific

I would tighten the offer into something like this:

- Free site assessment
- Fixed quote after inspection
- Soft-wash treatment tailored to render type
- Optional biocide protection
- Clear explanation of what result to expect and how long it should last

That is much stronger than a generic "Get a quote" approach.

### 5. Improve the lead form

The form should ask for:

- name
- phone
- email
- postcode
- property type
- number of storeys
- render type if known
- where the staining is worst
- access issues
- photo upload
- preferred contact method

This would materially improve lead quality.

### 6. Rework the page architecture

If you keep this under SWC, I would build a render-cleaning content cluster:

- main render cleaning service page
- render cleaning gallery page
- render cleaning FAQ section
- 3 to 6 area-led pages for towns where you actually want the work
- 2 case-study pages

That is more useful than launching a thin separate site.

### 7. Use bundles intelligently

One of the strongest commercial advantages of keeping it under SWC is bundling.

Natural bundle offers:

- render + fascias/soffits
- render + gutter clearing
- render + conservatory roof
- render + pressure washing for surrounding surfaces

That raises average order value and helps the job feel like a full exterior refresh rather than a one-off wall clean.

### 8. Tighten the CTA strategy

The current render-cleaning idea is quote-led, which is correct. But the CTA should be sharper:

- Book a free render assessment
- Get a fixed quote after site visit
- Send photos for a fast initial view

Those are more natural than a generic contact CTA.

## My Recommended Direction

### Option A: Best option now

Keep render cleaning inside SWC and make it feel specialist.

This is the lowest-risk and highest-return move.

Why:

- strongest trust position
- strongest SEO base
- easiest operationally
- best cross-sell economics
- fastest to improve

### Option B: Middle ground

Create a specialist sub-brand presentation without splitting the company.

Example:

`Somerset Render Cleaning`
`A specialist service from Somerset Window Cleaning`

This gives you niche positioning while preserving the trust stack.

### Option C: Separate brand later

Only do this after you have:

- real render photo library
- dedicated render testimonials
- consistent render lead flow
- a repeatable quoting and delivery process
- enough demand to justify its own site and brand overhead

Right now you do not have enough evidence in the project files to justify that jump.

## Bottom Line

Yes, I think the render cleaning idea can be improved.

My improvement is not mainly a prettier site. It is a better commercial structure:

- keep the trust and authority of SWC
- make render cleaning look and feel like a specialist vertical
- build around proof, not placeholders
- qualify leads better
- only split into a separate brand if the numbers later justify it

If you want the blunt version:

The separate-brand idea is interesting, but it is weaker than an upgraded SWC specialist vertical. I would not spend time launching it as a standalone site yet.
